About Me

Fred Xu

Ph.D. student in Computer Science, University of California, Los Angeles

EmailGoogle ScholarGitHubLinkedInRésumé (PDF)

I’m a Computer Science Ph.D. student at UCLA. I research probabilistic generative modeling (flow matching and diffusion), graph learning and uncertainty quantification, with applications to finance and science. My first- and co-first-author papers have appeared at NeurIPS, KDD and ACL, and I review for NeurIPS, ICML and ICLR.

Alongside the Ph.D. I’ve done industry research at Block, across two AI research internships, and at Hitachi and Amazon, applying this work to financial fraud detection, customer risk modeling, transaction foundation models and recommendation. Before graduate school I was a software engineer at NortonLifeLock, building security and privacy products.

Beyond the technical work, my interests run to AI and society, the philosophy of AI and continental philosophy, and on this site I write about those subjects, about literature, and about the papers behind my research.

Experience

Jan – Sep 2026

AI Research Intern

Block, Inc. · Oakland, CA

  • Function-space flow matching. Proposed kernel Functional Flow Matching (kFFM), which replaces independent prior–data pairing with entropic optimal transport under a kernel-induced cost, with well-posedness and discretization-invariance guarantees; improved distributional matching over FFM, diffusion and OT baselines on time-series and PDE benchmarks. First-author paper at NeurIPS 2026.
  • Graph anomaly detection for fraud. Developed EB-GAD, a training-free, label-free anomaly detector that fits a graph prior by empirical Bayes and scores nodes by closed-form finite-horizon control energy; best or tied-best AUROC on 9 of 11 benchmarks, including four financial fraud networks of up to 3.7M nodes, with margins of up to 21.7 points. First-author paper at NeurIPS 2026.
  • Uncertainty quantification for GNNs. Proposed DSS-GNN, which expands node embeddings on graph-spectral and Wiener-chaos axes so a single representation supports calibrated prediction, out-of-distribution detection and distribution-shift robustness. First-author paper at NeurIPS 2026.
  • Graph generation. Co-developed FlowPhon, which generates dense graphs via flow matching on graphon operators; second-author paper under review.
  • Transaction foundation model. Built a GPT-style foundation model over customer transaction event sequences to model Cash App balance changes.
